Lexicon

A language for reasoning stability.

The Reflective Alignment Lexicon gives AI reasoning failures stable names, identifiers, and boundaries.
It is a governed ontology for describing observable patterns of coherence, reflection, drift, and failure in AI reasoning — without claiming to read model internals or certify safety.

Core Insight

Before AI reasoning can be governed, its failures need stable names.

Problem

AI vocabulary is unstable.

Words like alignment, hallucination, drift, trust, and failure are used across papers, policies, products, and audits — but they often mean different things in each context.

01

Different words

The same reasoning pattern may be described with different labels across studies or tools.

02

Same word

The same label may be used for structurally different behaviors.

03

No stable ID

Without canonical identifiers, concepts are hard to compare, cite, or reconstruct over time.

04

No boundary

Without diagnostic boundaries, reviewers can disagree about what a failure actually is.

What it does not claim.

Lexicon mapping produces ontological candidates for human review. It does not prove model state, certify safety, measure truth, access hidden model internals, or replace expert judgment.

FAQ

Boundaries by design.

The Lexicon is intentionally narrow. Its value is structural clarity, not overclaiming.

Does it read model weights?

No. The Lexicon is designed around observable artifacts, not hidden model internals.

Does it certify safety?

No. It provides diagnostic vocabulary and candidate mappings for review, not safety certification.

Is it a term browser?

Not publicly yet. The full canonical registry remains controlled until public release.

Is the Mapper the Lexicon?

No. The Lexicon is the ontology. The Mapper is a local research instrument that applies it to documents.
