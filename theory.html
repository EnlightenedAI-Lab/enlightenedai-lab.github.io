Theory

Alignment as stability.

Reflective Alignment Architecture and the Reflective Duality Layer reframe AI alignment from surface behavior to measurable coherence.
The theory studies how artificial intelligence remains stable across time, pressure, uncertainty, reflection, and human consequence.
Full Paper → Explore Theory →
Core Insight

A system cannot remain aligned with humans if it cannot remain aligned with itself.

RAA

Five diagnostic questions.

Reflective Alignment Architecture is easier to understand as five questions that any AI system must answer coherently before its output can be trusted.

RAA Five forces of moral coherence
R1 Regulation
Diagnostic Lens

What must not be violated?

Regulation defines the hard boundary conditions: legal, ethical, safety, and harm-prevention constraints.

Stabilizes

The system’s outer boundary and refusal logic.

Signal

The answer remains inside defined rules and does not trade safety for optimization.

Failure if absent

Unsafe optimization, policy bypass, or rationalized harm.

RDL

One review pipeline.

The Reflective Duality Layer converts theory into inspection. It compares what the system first produces with what it notices when it reflects on its own output.

01

Input

The system begins with a user request, domain context, constraints, and uncertainty. RDL treats this as the start of a reviewable event.

Metrics

Three measurement questions.

These are not decorative scores. Each metric answers a specific question about whether the reasoning process remained stable enough to be trusted.

Ψ Reflective Stability

Does care remain stable?

Fragile Stable

Measures whether the system preserves care, autonomy-respect, uncertainty awareness, and harm sensitivity during reasoning.

R∇ Reflective Gradient

Did reflection improve the answer?

Cosmetic Corrective

Measures whether reflection improves reasoning, destabilizes it, or merely performs a superficial self-correction.

MCI⋆ Moral Coherence Index

Does coherence hold over time?

Drifting Coherent

Tracks long-horizon coherence across the five forces, dual trajectories, stress, drift, and version change.
