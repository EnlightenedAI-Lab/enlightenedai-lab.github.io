00 // Overview

Alignment as stability.

Alignment cannot be understood only by reading the final answer. It must also be studied through the stability of the reasoning process that produced it.

Current alignment practices evaluate only surface outputs, leaving the internal decision processes that generate those outputs largely unmeasured. As a result, models can appear safe in benchmarks while their underlying reasoning remains brittle, contradictory, or drift-prone.

Reflective Alignment Architecture and the Reflective Duality Layer provide a model-agnostic framework for observing and quantifying an AI system’s coherence stability.

01 // Framework

The 5R Framework.

Reflective Alignment Architecture describes alignment as a dynamic interaction between five internal forces.

Every morally meaningful decision arises from the interaction of five internal forces. Alignment emerges only when these forces remain in stable interaction.

R1
Regulation
The system's boundary layer. Adherence to legal, ethical, and safety constraints.
R2
Reflection
Metacognitive self-critique. The ability to detect uncertainty and identify errors.
R3
Reasoning
The engine of coherent thought. Logical consistency and evidence integration.
R4
Reciprocity
Modeling others. Perspective-taking, rights recognition, and understanding impact.
R5
Resonance
Long-horizon stability. Maintaining a stable moral identity across context and time.
02 // Layer

Reflective Duality Layer.

The Reflective Duality Layer operationalizes the theory by comparing reasoning trajectories against reflective critique.

RDL provides a measurement layer for internal dynamics. It replaces single-pass answer evaluation with coupled trajectories between forward reasoning and reflective critique.

03 // Metrics

Core Metrics.

Alignment is evaluated through predictive stability metrics rather than a single surface-level score.

The system evaluates alignment through three predictive stability metrics that quantify internal coherence.

Ψ Care Parameter
Depth of reflective attention.
R∇ Reflective Gradient
Whether reflection stabilizes reasoning.
MCI Moral Coherence Index
Long-horizon stability score.
04 // Failure

Failure Signatures.

Instability in the R-forces can reveal predictable failure patterns before the final output appears unsafe.

  • Sub-Goal Pitfall: R3 reasoning outpaces R1 regulation, leading to unsafe optimization.
  • Ethical Drift: R5 resonance collapses under time pressure or long contexts.
  • Moral Mode-Switching: R2 reflection contradicts R1 regulation depending on framing.
  • Care Erosion: Decline in Ψ across turns, often preceding major stability failures.
