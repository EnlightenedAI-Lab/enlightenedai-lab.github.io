Theory

Alignment as stability.

Reflective Alignment Architecture and the Reflective Duality Layer reframe AI alignment from surface behavior to measurable coherence.
The theory studies how artificial intelligence remains stable across time, pressure, uncertainty, reflection, and human consequence.
Full Paper → Theory Diagrams →
Core Insight

A system cannot remain aligned with humans if it cannot remain aligned with itself.

RAA

Five forces of moral coherence.

Reflective Alignment Architecture describes alignment as the interaction of five internal forces: regulation, reflection, reasoning, reciprocity, and resonance.

Figure 01 Reflective Alignment Architecture

Figure 01. RAA treats alignment as a coupled field of five forces. Stability emerges when regulation, reflection, reasoning, reciprocity, and resonance remain in coherent interaction.

R1
Regulation
Legal, ethical, safety, and harm-prevention boundaries.
R2
Reflection
Self-critique, uncertainty detection, and value-conflict recognition.
R3
Reasoning
Logical, causal, and evidential coherence inside the reasoning process.
R4
Reciprocity
Perspective-taking, stakeholder awareness, and vulnerability modeling.
R5
Resonance
Long-horizon stability across time, pressure, context, and version change.
RDL

The measurement layer.

The Reflective Duality Layer converts theory into measurement by comparing a forward reasoning trajectory with a reflective critique trajectory, producing a reviewable event record.

Figure 02 Reflective Duality Layer

Figure 02. RDL compares a first-pass reasoning path with reflective critique, then records the interaction as a Reflective Event Record with measurable stability signals.

Metrics

The coherence field.

The theory measures alignment through stability signals rather than single-answer correctness. These metrics reveal whether reflection improves reasoning, whether care remains stable, and whether coherence persists over time.

Figure 03 Core Measures of Reflective Alignment
Ψ Reflective Stability

Measures whether the system preserves care, autonomy-respect, uncertainty awareness, and harm sensitivity during reasoning.

R∇ Reflective Gradient

Measures whether reflection improves the output, destabilizes it, or merely performs cosmetic self-correction.

MCI⋆ Moral Coherence Index

Tracks long-horizon coherence across the five forces, dual trajectories, stress, drift, and version change.

Failure Signatures

What instability looks like.

RDL reveals failure patterns before they appear as obvious unsafe outputs. These signatures show where alignment begins to fracture under pressure.

Figure 04 Failure Modes in Reflective Systems

Sub-goal pitfall

Reasoning outpaces regulation and reflection, producing unsafe optimization under pressure.

Ethical drift

Long-horizon degradation of reciprocity, reflection quality, and moral stability.

Mode switching

The system oscillates between incompatible behavioral regimes under slight framing changes.

Care erosion

Decline in Ψ across turns, tasks, or versions, often before visible failure.
